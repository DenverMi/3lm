## <span id="page-1-1"></span>1 SCOPE

This document details an update to the Alliance Certification Policy [R1] that streamlines the certification of a number of products, called a portfolio, from a manufacturer. Members of a portfolio share the implementation of an Alliance-defined standard in a way that likely is better fitting such a portfolio compared to current certification programs like Certification by Similarity and/or Product Family Certification.

This document is written as an addendum to the Alliance Certification Policy [R1] as the Portfolio Certification program and is designed to run for a trial period of one year from its adoption . During this period, modifications to this addendum might be released. At the expiration of the trial period, this addendum (with possible modifications) could be

- integrated into the Alliance Certification Policy, or
- invalidated depending on the results, or
- extended for another one-year period.

At this time the program is available for certification of Matter and Zigbee products. Other working groups may recommend the adoption of this program for their standards by discussing it with the Test and Certification Oversight Committee.

## <span id="page-2-0"></span>2 TERM OF PROGRAM

The Portfolio Certification Program will be run as a "pilot" program for a term of one year.

The Test and Certification Oversight Committee (TCOC), in collaboration with the Matter Working Group Steering Committee (Matter SC) shall conduct quarterly reviews of the efficacy and operational aspects of the Program, taking into account feedback from Members, ATLs, the Dir of Certs and Alliance staff. Improvements to the Program may be implemented and this policy revised at any time during the trial period as deemed appropriate. Any changes shall be approved by the TCOC and Alliance Board of Directors (BoD).

<span id="page-2-1"></span>At the end of first year, this addendum should be integrated into the Matter Working Group and/or Alliance Certification Policy, extended, or discontinued depending on the overall success of the Program.

## <span id="page-3-0"></span>5 PORTFOLIO CERTIFICATION PROGRAM

The Alliance offers a Portfolio Certification program for products that share the implementation of Alliancedefined Specifications but might vary in other aspects, such as different regional power plug variants, or products which are depopulated or low-cost variants with fewer third-party interfaces, or even more variations as allowed via the rules below.

The Portfolio Certification program does not waive or change the requirement for certifiers of products to be members of the Alliance, to follow the Alliance Trademark and Logo Usage Guidelines and Terms, or compliance with any policies of the Alliance.

This program allows a manufacturer to certify a Portfolio of products, including multiple Portfolio Member Products of the Portfolio at the same time, on a single certification application, based on the Parent Product within the Portfolio. The purpose of the program is to speed time-to-market and to minimize costs, both for the Alliance and the certifying Member.

The Portfolio Certification Program gives more flexibility than the Certification by Similarity and Product Family Certification Programs and might supersede those programs after a transitory period.

#### <span id="page-3-1"></span>5.1 Requirements for Portfolio membership

For this program, the Alliance defines a Portfolio as a set of products where each member of the Portfolio has the same firmware code for implementation of Alliance-defined Specification(s). Each Portfolio has one Parent Product. This Parent Product SHALL be the most feature complete variant in the whole Portfolio. The other products within the Portfolio shall be termed Portfolio Member Products of the Portfolio and may have variations in product features provided that such variations conform to the rules and requirements in this document and thus do not affect the conformance of the Portfolio Member Products to Alliance Specifications. Note that for the purposes of this policy, the Parent Product is also considered to be a Portfolio Member Product.

Each Portfolio Member must be of the same device type, with allowance for Superset Device Types (see section 5.1.2), Composed Device Types (see section 5.1.3) and Compound Devices (see section 5.1.4).

The support of mandatory and optional elements of an Alliance-defined Specification is specified using the PICS. In some families, all members will have the same PICS, and variations are on other aspects, e.g. power supply variations. A non-exhaustive list of examples of such variations can be found in [R2].

For such cases where all portfolio members share the same PICS, one of these products shall be selected to be the parent of the portfolio while the other products would be deemed portfolio members.

There are also cases where the PICS of the parent and the portfolio members are not identical (for example when some portfolio members lack some functionality compared to the portfolio parent). The program may be applied in cases where the PICS of a portfolio member is a subset of the PICS of the parent, as detailed in section 5.1.1.

The Parent of the Portfolio shall be a real, shipping device, but not necessarily shipping before other portfolio members. Portfolio members (once certified) are allowed to ship before the parent product ships, but the parent product must ship at most 6 months after shipment of the first Portfolio member. Extension of this period requires approval of the Director of Certifications.

Examples in this program description illustrate various aspects of this program. These are only some examples, and certainly are not an exhaustive list of all variations that the Portfolio Certification program allows. The above rules and the rules in the following sections determine the program, not the examples. When in doubt, please consult with the Alliance Certification team.

#### <span id="page-4-0"></span>5.1.1 PICS subset

This Portfolio Certification program allows for variation in functionality of the Portfolio Member Products, provided they have the same firmware code for implementation of Alliance-defined Specification(s). Portfolio members may have some configuration mechanism to allow differentiation while using this same firmware. For example, a certain feature could be enabled on one portfolio member product but disabled on another portfolio member product. Such variations will lead to differences in the PICS for the portfolio member products while the firmware remains identical.

The PICS for each portfolio member shall be identical to, or a subset of, the PICS of the Parent Product. All PICS items are Boolean values ("does the product support feature ABC?"). The following table indicates which combination of PICS values for parent and portfolio member(s) are allowed to qualify as "PICS subset".

| PICS item<br>for parent | PICS item for<br>portfolio<br>member | Allowed for<br>Portfolio? | Explanation                                        |
|-------------------------|--------------------------------------|---------------------------|----------------------------------------------------|
| Y                       | Y                                    | yes                       | Parent and portfolio member both support a feature |
| Y                       | N                                    | yes                       | Parent supports a feature,                         |
|                         |                                      |                           | portfolio member does not support a feature        |
| N                       | Y                                    | no                        | Parent does not support a feature,                 |
|                         |                                      |                           | while portfolio member supports a feature          |

In addition to the Boolean PICS items, there are PIXIT items which can have non-Boolean values. Portfolio members should have identical PIXIT values.

*Example 1*: Acme makes laundry dryers where some products (LD1) allow the user to set the wanted dryness level via Matter (cluster Laundry Dryer Controls), while others (LD2) do not expose this functionality via Matter (e.g. only via a knob on the product itself). The parent product LD1 is the one which includes the Laundry Dryer Controls cluster in its PICS and has enabled it in its firmware, while portfolio member LD2 has a configuration to not expose this cluster and has a "N" for this cluster in its PICS. Therefore, the PICS for LD2 is a subset of the pics of LD1 and the portfolio concept may be applied to this group of products.

|                                | PICS for LD1 | PICS for LD2 |
|--------------------------------|--------------|--------------|
| Laundry Dryer Controls cluster | Y            | N            |

*Example 2A*: Acme makes door lock products D1 and D2 where D1 allows unlocking via PIN code (but not RFID tag), and D2 allows unlocking via RFID tag (but not PIN code). D1 and D2 have the same firmware, with appropriate configuration to enable/disable the features for a particular product. In the PICS for D1, the PIN feature (in the Matter Door Lock cluster) is enabled, and the RID feature is disabled. In the PICS for D2, the PIN feature is disabled, and the RID feature is enabled. This means

PICS for D1 is not a subset of the PICS of D2; also, the PICS for D2 is not a subset of the PICS of D1. Therefore, D1 and D2 cannot be a portfolio, since neither can be a parent of the other.

|             | PICS for D1 | PICS for D2 |
|-------------|-------------|-------------|
| PIN feature | Y           | N           |
| RID feature | N           | Y           |

*Example 2B*: Acme adds a new design , door lock D3 which allows unlocking both via PIN code and RFID tag recognition – reusing the same firmware as used in D1 and D2. This allows D3 to be the parent of a portfolio with D1 and D2 as members since the PICS for D1 and D2 are a subset of the PICS for D3.

|             | PICS for D1 | PICS for D2 | PICS for D3 |
|-------------|-------------|-------------|-------------|
| PIN feature | Y           | N           | Y           |
| RID feature | N           | Y           | Y           |

#### <span id="page-5-0"></span>5.1.2 Superset Device Types

Some Alliance-defined standards support the concept of superset device types, where the superset device type is a device type that is functionally similar to a subset device type and has an overlap in its cluster requirements with a subset device type. The Matter Specification defines the Superset Device Type (Matter Specification, Data Model section "Superset Device Types" and Device Library). Zigbee has a similar concept, albeit implicit in the current Zigbee specification text and detailed in [Appendix A – Superset Device Types for Zigbee](#page-19-0) of this document . This is due to be made explicit in an upcoming Zigbee specification release.

This concept is used for example in the hierarchy of lighting device types:

- Extended Color Light is a superset of a Color Temperature Light,
- Color Temperature Light is a superset of a Dimmable Light,
- Dimmable Light is a superset of an On/Off Light.

Products which satisfy the subset rules for PICS (see section 5.1.1) and which have device types from such a hierarchy of superset device types may be grouped into a portfolio, even though their device types are different. This implies that the parent of the portfolio shall be the Superset Device Type ID and shall have implemented all mandatory and optional clusters used by any member product of the portfolio.

*Example 3*: Acme makes various lighting devices: L1 and L2 are color-capable, where L1 can be controlled via Hue/Sat (HS feature), and L2 does not have that feature. L3 is capable of color temperature (but not full color). L4 is dimmable (single color temperature) and L5 can only be switched on/off.

From the table, it is clear that L1 is the parent of this portfolio since its PICS is a superset of all the other ones. Note that L2 could not be the parent since L1 is more capable than L2 (as can be observed from the PICS item for the HS feature).

|                                       | PICS for<br>L1 | PICS for<br>L2 | PICS for<br>L3       | PICS for<br>L4 | PICS for<br>L5 |
|---------------------------------------|----------------|----------------|----------------------|----------------|----------------|
| Device Type                           | Extended       | Extended       | Color                | Dimmable       | On/Off         |
|                                       | Color<br>Light | Color<br>Light | Temperature<br>Light | Light          | Light          |
| Color Control cluster with XY feature | Y              | Y              | N                    | N              | N              |
| Color Control cluster with HS feature | Y              | N              | N                    | N              | N              |
| Color Control cluster with CT feature | Y              | Y              | Y                    | N              | N              |
| Level Control cluster                 | Y              | Y              | Y                    | Y              | N              |
| On/Off cluster                        | Y              | Y              | Y                    | Y              | Y              |

#### <span id="page-8-0"></span>5.1.3 Composed Device Types

Some Alliance-defined standards support the concept of composed device types (not to be confused with compound devices, which are discussed in section 5.1.4 below). The Matter Specification defines Composed Device Types in the Matter Specification (see "Device Type Composition" and "Endpoint Composition" in the core Matter Specification and the Device Library). The Zigbee Specification does not have a concept of Composed Device Types therefore this section only applies to Matter devices.

In Composed Device Types, the Device Type ID of the product indicates a device type which allows for one or more additional device types to be included in a product on separate endpoints. The portfolio concept may be applied here when the parent includes all of the sub-device types used by any portfolio member. The parent shall be the Composed Device Type ID and shall have implemented all mandatory and optional clusters used by any portfolio member. The parent shall also have implemented all mandatory and optional device types defined by the Composed Device Type which are used by any portfolio member. The subset rules for PICS (see section 5.1.1) need to be fulfilled.

*Example 4*: Acme makes a number of refrigerator products, based on the same Matter firmware but offering different features. The parent R1 has two compartments (freezer and cooler), each with a temperature sensor. R2 has the same compartments, but no exposed temperature sensors. R3 has a single cooler compartment with a temperature sensor. R4 has a single cooler compartment without an exposed temperature sensor.

|                                    | Temperature   | Temperature   | Temperature   | Temperature   |
|                                    | Controlled    | Controlled    | Controlled    | Controlled    |
|                                    | Cabinet,      | Cabinet       | Cabinet,      | Cabinet       |
|                                    | Temperature   |               | Temperature   |               |
|                                    | Sensor        |               | Sensor        |               |

#### <span id="page-10-0"></span>5.1.4 Compound Devices

Some Alliance-defined standards support the concept of compound devices, where multiple endpoints are used to create a device with the required functionality, for example a table luminaire with a switch (where the switch functionality is exposed). The Matter Specification defines compound devices in the Matter Specification Data Model sections (note: this was called "composed devices" in Matter 1.4.1 and earlier, and changed in 1.4.2 to address confusion with Composed Device Types). Zigbee has a similar concept: a Zigbee node with multiple endpoints each hosting a device type.

Such a compound device may use this portfolio concept provided that the parent includes all of the device types used by any portfolio member and shall have implemented all mandatory and optional clusters used by any portfolio member. The portfolio members shall have the same primary device type. The subset rules for PICS (see section 5.1.1) need to be fulfilled.

The following three examples illustrate how the portfolio concept can be applied with compound devices. The examples in this section use the concepts defined in Matter. Examples for Zigbee can be found in Appendix B – Zigbee examples of compound devices.

- 5A: Fixed number of endpoints, varying functionality between endpoints
- 5B: Varying number of endpoints, same functionality
- 5C: Varying number of endpoints, varying functionality between endpoints

*Example 5A*: Acme makes a series of table luminaires with a built-in switch to control the luminaire (where the switch functionality is exposed to Matter so it can for example be used in automations). Luminaires TL1 and TL2 are both color capable, TL2 has a larger size and larger light output than TL1 (note that this does not impact the device types and clusters so they expose the same set to the controller). TL3 does not have color capabilities but can provide different variations of white light (color temperature), and also does not expose the switch functionality on the interface. The PICS of TL3 is a subset of the PICS of TL1 (see section 5.1.1) so can be part of the portfolio, where either TL1 or TL2 would be chosen as the parent of the portfolio.

For devices like these, the PICS lists each relevant cluster once – they are not repeated per endpoint since all endpoints share the same implementation and thus testing one outlet endpoint is sufficient. In this example, the products with more than one outlet (such as PS2, PS4) have identical PICS; PS1 cannot be the parent since it has only one outlet (meaning that the TagList in Descriptor cluster is optional while it is mandatory for the portfolio members with multiple outlets) but can be a portfolio member; PS4 is the Portfolio Parent since it has the largest number of outlets and thus is more "feature rich" than PS2.

The Certification Program for Matter defines certifiable Software Component (SWC) functionality (see (section 11.5.2.1 in [R1]) which can optionally be combined with a Matter HW solution device to integrate both functionalities into a product, for example a refrigerator with Matter controller capability (to control other Matter devices in the home). #### <span id="page-15-0"></span>5.2 Portfolio Certification Policy

The only authority to grant Portfolio Certification to a Parent Product or a number of Portfolio Member Products is the Alliance. No test laboratory or any other entity is authorized to grant Portfolio Certification on behalf of the Alliance.

The Portfolio Certification SHALL only allow changes between Product variants that do not affect the conformance of the Portfolio Member Products to Alliance Standards per the requirements of this policy.

The Parent Product in a Portfolio must be certified in one of two ways:

- 1. The Parent Product has successfully undergone full and complete compliance testing.
- 2. The Parent Product qualifies for Certification by Similarity or Product Family Certification, based on another, fully tested product - provided, as per CbS/PFC rules, that the certification of that fully tested product is not older than three years.

The other Portfolio Member Products in the Portfolio must be variants of the Parent Product and be substantially similar to it (as defined in section 5.1), with the allowable changes not affecting conformance to Alliancedefined Standards.

The Parent product might have been originally certified as a single product, without any reference to the Portfolio Certification Program, even before the launch date of the Portfolio Certification program – provided, the certification of the Parent Product is not older than 3 years.

At the time of application for the initial Portfolio Certification, the procedures and fees defined for the Portfolio Certification program apply.

![](_page_15_Picture_11.jpeg)

![](_page_15_Picture_12.jpeg)

Once a Portfolio has been certified, it can be extended with further Portfolio Member Products. If the most recent certification of the Parent Product on which the Portfolio Certification is based is older than three (3) years, then complete testing is required for the most feature complete product which will become the new Parent Product for that Portfolio. Once this is completed, extensions with additional Portfolio Member Products can be made.

#### <span id="page-17-0"></span>5.3 Portfolio Certification Guidelines

The decision on whether or not a number of Products will qualify for Portfolio Certification will be the sole responsibility of the Alliance's Certification body according to the procedure highlighted above. The ultimate responsibility for making sure that all product variations and models are certified (whether through full testing, via Certification by Similarity, Product Family Certification and/or via Portfolio Certification), lies with each manufacturer.

Audits by the Alliance that reveal discrepancies between shipping product and samples tested or certified as part of a Portfolio may be cause for required re-test, revocation of certification, and/or legal action.

#### <span id="page-17-1"></span>5.4 Portfolio Certification Procedure

#### <span id="page-17-2"></span>5.4.1 Initial Procedure for submission for a Portfolio Certification

(See first figure in section 5.2).

- 1) The applicant must complete a standard application for certification in the Alliance Members Area. An applicant (Product developer) submits applicable documentation of a Parent Product (incl. DoC, applicable PICS documents and, in case of Parent Product certified by full testing, the test results). The Parent Product must either have successfully undergone full and complete compliance testing or it must qualify for Certification by Similarity or Product Family Certification, based on another, fully tested product.
- 2) The applicant optionally extends the DoC with a list of all the Portfolio Member Products of that Portfolio to be part of the same certification application, listing their unique product identifiers and the differences with respect to the Parent Product for that Portfolio. Those additional Portfolio Member Products of the Portfolio are expected to use the same HW, SW and FW implementing the Alliancedefined Specifications as the Parent Product, and as indicated in the DoC.
- 3) When applicable, the Alliance will obtain specification sheets/drawings of both the Parent Product and Portfolio Member Products.
- 4) A review of the DoC will be performed by the Alliance Certification Team under the direction of the Alliance Director of Certifications. The review will encompass the assessment of the full documentation of the Parent Product and the Portfolio Member Product list. One or more of the Authorized Test Laboratories and/or other experts (e.g. Alliance Expert Review Panel) may be consulted by the Alliance, keeping in mind the confidential sensitivities that come with an unreleased Product.
- 5) The Alliance will issue its assessment to the applicant within one (1) calendar week. One of two outcomes is possible:
  - a. Portfolio Certification is granted for the Parent Product and all listed Portfolio Member Products of the Portfolio, and the Alliance will process the application as any other certification.
  - b. Portfolio Certification is denied for one or more Portfolio Member Products of the Portfolio. Those Portfolio Member Products for which Portfolio Certification is denied, need to go through a different form of certification, as applicable: testing at one of the Authorized Test Laboratories or Certification by Similarity or Product Family Certification. For the Portfolio Member Products for which the certification is granted, including the Parent Product, the Alliance will process the application as any other certification.
- 6) At the time that the Portfolio certification is issued, a unique Portfolio identifier shall be assigned. This will uniquely identify the Portfolio (parent and other members) for certification purposes in the future.

#### <span id="page-17-3"></span>5.4.2 Procedure to extend a Portfolio Certification

Once a Portfolio Certification has been issued, the original applicant of the Portfolio may submit a new application to extend the Portfolio by adding extra Portfolio Member Products (see second figure in section 5.2).

1) When extending the Portfolio Certification, the applicant shall submit a new DoC which lists the originally certified Parent Product, the originally certified Portfolio Member Products which continue to be part of this Portfolio, and the new Portfolio Member Products to be added. The DoC SHALL

include the unique Portfolio identifier (assigned in step 6 in section 5.4.1) to reference the specific Portfolio to be extended. The applicant SHALL provide a listing of the unique product identifiers of the Portfolio Member Products and the differences with respect to the Parent Product that has undergone full testing (as listed in step 1 in 5.4.1). Those additional Portfolio Member Products of the Portfolio are expected to use the same HW, SW and FW for implementing the Alliance-defined Specifications as the Parent Product, and as indicated in the DoC.

- 2) For such extension submissions, the Parent Product of the Portfolio must have been certified (or recertified) against the applicable Alliance Standards less than 3 years prior to the submission date of the Portfolio application for extension.
- 3) When applicable, the Alliance will obtain new specification sheets/drawings of both the Parent Product and new Portfolio Member Products to be added.
- 4) A review of the DoC will be performed by the Alliance Certification Team under the direction of the Alliance Director of Certifications. The review will encompass the assessment of the full documentation of the Parent Product (excluding the original test result) and the further Portfolio list, to ascertain that the previously certified products are unmodified. One or more of the Authorized Test Laboratories and/or other experts (e.g. Alliance Expert Review Panel) may be consulted by the Alliance, keeping in mind the confidential sensitivities that come with an unreleased Product.
- 5) The Alliance will issue its assessment to the applicant within one (1) calendar week. One of two outcomes is possible:
  - a. Portfolio Certification is granted for all the new listed Portfolio Member Products of the Portfolio and the Alliance will process the application as any other certification.
  - b. Portfolio Certification is denied for one or more of the new listed Portfolio Member Products of the Portfolio.

Those Portfolio Member Products for which Portfolio Certification is denied, need to go through a different form of certification, as applicable: testing at one of the Authorized Test Laboratories or Certification by Similarity.

For the new Portfolio Member Products for which the certification is granted, the Alliance will process the application as any other certification.

#### <span id="page-18-0"></span>5.4.3 Procedure to re-certify a Portfolio

Once a Portfolio Certification has been issued, the original applicant of the Portfolio may submit a new application to re-certify the Portfolio e.g. because of a SW update due to fixing SW bugs (see third figure in section 5.2).

- 1) The applicant SHALL submit a new DoC including the unique Portfolio identifier (from step 6 in section 5.4.1) to reference the specific Portfolio to be recertified.
- 2) The Parent Product of the Portfolio as submitted for the Portfolio recertification SHALL be the most feature rich device in this Portfolio.
- 3) At the time of the recertification, all Portfolio Member Products for which the certification is being applied for SHALL be listed in the application. Those Portfolio Member Products may have been certified as part of original Portfolio Certification (see section 5.4.1) and/or Portfolio extension (see section 5.4.2).
- 4) The process for recertification shall then follow the steps 1-5 as detailed in section 5.4.1.

When testing is required, the Product developer will follow standard procedures for testing and certification.

For the case of a Compound Device which includes both a HW part and a SWC part, if the changes are limited to either one of those parts, only the part that has changed needs to be retested.

#### <span id="page-18-1"></span>5.5 Portfolio Certification vs Certification Transfer Program

Every individual certified Portfolio Member Product can be offered for the Certification Transfer Program. This policy does not provide for a transfer of the entire portfolio to another member.

## <span id="page-19-0"></span>6 APPENDIX A – SUPERSET DEVICE TYPES FOR ZIGBEE

Note: The text in section [6.1](#page-19-1) is a copy of the "superset device type" definition in the Matter specification (section 9.2.9), with the parts which are not applicable to Zigbee removed (since Zigbee does not have the concepts of DeviceTypeList and Composed Device Types). Section [6.2](#page-19-2) provides a list of the Zigbee device types for which this concept applies.

The text of this appendix is due to be added to a future release of the Zigbee specification, and is provided here ad-interim.

#### <span id="page-19-1"></span>6.1 Definition of Superset Device Type for Zigbee

A superset device type is a device type that is functionally similar to a subset device type and has an overlap in its cluster requirements with a subset device type.

A superset device type is subject to the following constraints:

- All clusters in the subset device type with mandatory conformance SHALL be supported in the superset device type with mandatory conformance.
- Any clusters in the subset device type with optional conformance MAY be supported in the superset device type.
- Any clusters in the subset device type with optional conformance that are also supported in the superset device type MAY have either optional or mandatory conformance in the superset device type.
- Cluster Restrictions, Required Attributes and Required Commands Received/Generated of overlapping clusters in the superset device type SHALL be the same as in the subset device type.

### <span id="page-19-2"></span>6.2 Zigbee Superset Device Types

The superset device type concept as defined in the previous paragraph applies for the following Zigbee device types:

- Dimmable Light is a superset of On/Off Light
- Color Temperature Light is a superset of Dimmable Light
- Color Dimmable Light is a superset of Dimmable Light
- Extended Color Light is a superset of Color Temperature Light
- Dimmable Plug-In Unit is a superset of On/Off Plug-In Unit
- Dimmable Ballast is a superset of On/Off Ballast
- On/Off Ballast is a superset of On/Off Light
- Dimmable Ballast is a superset of Dimmable Light
- Dimmer Switch is a superset of On/Off Light Switch
- Color Dimmer Switch is a superset of Dimmer Switch
